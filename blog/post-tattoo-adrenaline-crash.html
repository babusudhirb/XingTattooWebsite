Post-Tattoo Crash: Adrenaline, Fatigue and First-Tattoo Emotions

Peace (Chingri Awungshi)28 Oct 2026 8 min read

Feeling drained, shaky, cold or emotional after a tattoo is common and usually temporary. It's often called the post-tattoo crash: your body releases adrenaline and endorphins during the session, then they wear off. Eat a proper meal, drink water, rest and keep warm. If you feel faint, feverish or unwell for more than a day, see a doctor.

Key takeaways

  • Tiredness, shivering, shakiness or feeling low after a session is common, especially after a first or long tattoo.
  • It's linked to the adrenaline and endorphins your body releases during tattooing wearing off.
  • Eat before your session, hydrate, and bring a sweet snack and water to the studio.
  • Early doubts or 'did I make a mistake?' feelings in the first days are normal and usually pass as the tattoo heals.
  • Fainting, fever, chills that don't settle or spreading redness need a doctor, not just rest.

You've just walked out of the studio with your first tattoo. You're thrilled, you can't stop looking at it — and then, a couple of hours later, you feel completely wiped out. Maybe you're shivery. Maybe you're oddly emotional. Maybe you lie awake wondering if you made a mistake.

If that sounds familiar, you're not alone. Many people call it the post-tattoo crash. This guide explains why it happens, what's normal, how to recover quickly, and when tiredness or feeling unwell is a reason to see a doctor.

This is general information, not medical advice. If you're worried about how you feel, speak to a doctor.

What is the post-tattoo crash?#

The post-tattoo crash is the drained, shaky or low feeling some people get after a session, once the adrenaline and endorphins released during tattooing wear off. It's usually temporary.

People describe it as:

  • Sudden tiredness or sleepiness.
  • Feeling cold, shivery or shaky.
  • Light-headedness, especially when standing up.
  • Low mood, tearfulness or feeling "flat".
  • Restlessness or trouble sleeping that night.

It tends to be stronger after a first tattoo, a long session, or a session on an empty stomach.

Why does a tattoo cause an adrenaline rush?#

Because your body treats the needle as a small, repeated injury. It responds with stress hormones like adrenaline and natural painkillers called endorphins, which keep you alert and take the edge off the sensation.

During the session, that can feel like:

  • A buzz of energy or excitement.
  • Feeling more talkative or giggly than usual.
  • A slightly raised heart rate.
  • Pain that seems to fade after the first few minutes.

When the session ends, those hormones settle. The comedown is what many people feel as the crash.

What makes the crash worse?#

Long sessions, an empty stomach, dehydration, poor sleep and alcohol all make the crash stronger. Nerves before a first tattoo can add to it too.

What should I bring to my tattoo session?#

Bring water, a sweet snack and something to keep you comfortable. Small things make a big difference to how you feel during and after.

Session bag checklist:

  • A bottle of water.
  • A sweet snack — fruit, juice, glucose biscuits or a chocolate.
  • A light jacket or shawl in case you feel cold.
  • Headphones for longer sessions.
  • Loose, comfortable clothing that gives easy access to the area.
  • Photo ID — you must be 18 or older.

Peace's tip: If you start to feel light-headed during a session, say so straight away. We'll stop, lean you back, and get some sugar and water into you. It passes quickly — but only if you tell me early.

How do I recover from the post-tattoo crash?#

Eat, drink, rest and keep warm. Most people feel back to normal after a good meal and a night's sleep.

Recovery routine for the evening:

  1. Eat a proper meal soon after your session.
  2. Drink plenty of water.
  3. Keep warm with a light layer.
  4. Skip the gym and alcohol.
  5. Rest — an early night helps.
  6. Clean and care for the tattoo as instructed.

The next day:

  • Eat regular meals and stay hydrated.
  • Take it easy if you still feel tired.
  • Follow your aftercare routine.

Is it normal to feel regret or anxiety after a tattoo?#

Yes, early doubts are common, especially after a first or visible tattoo. Fresh tattoos are red, swollen and darker than they'll look once healed, so the first few days rarely show the final result.

Feelings people often have in the first days:

  • "It looks bigger than I expected."
  • "The lines look thick or blurry."
  • "What will my family or office think?"
  • "Did I choose the right design?"

What usually helps:

  • Wait for healing. Most tattoos look very different after a few weeks.
  • Look at healed photos of similar work.
  • Talk to someone you trust about how you feel.
  • Message your artist with photos if something looks wrong — not every worry needs to wait.

The surface of most tattoos heals in about 10 days to 3 weeks, and the ink settles fully over 1–3 months. Our guide to tattoo healing stages explains what to expect at each point.

What if the feeling doesn't pass?#

If doubts or low mood last long after the tattoo has healed, talk about it. Sometimes a small change — a touch-up, an added element, a rework — makes a big difference.

Options to discuss with your artist:

  • A touch-up if part of the tattoo healed unevenly.
  • Adding to the design so it feels more complete.
  • A rework or, in some cases, a cover-up. Our tattoo cover-up guide explains what's possible.

If you're feeling persistently low or anxious about more than the tattoo, speak to a doctor or a mental health professional.

When should I see a doctor?#

See a doctor if you faint or nearly faint, have a fever or chills that don't settle, feel increasingly unwell, or notice signs of infection around the tattoo.

Get medical advice promptly if you have:

  • Fainting, or feeling faint repeatedly.
  • A fever or chills that don't settle with rest.
  • Redness that spreads beyond the tattoo or keeps getting worse.
  • Pain or swelling that increases instead of easing.
  • Pus, a bad smell or red streaks from the tattoo.
  • Feeling generally unwell for more than a day.

Normal post-tattoo tiredness gets better with rest and food. Feeling worse over time is not normal. Our guide to infected tattoo signs explains warning signs in more detail.

Who is more likely to feel the crash?#

Anyone can, but it's more common in first-timers, people who are nervous about needles, and anyone with a long session or an empty stomach.

You may want to plan extra rest if:

  • It's your first tattoo.
  • The session is longer than a couple of hours.
  • You've had a stressful week or poor sleep.
  • You tend to feel faint with blood tests or injections.

Tell your artist beforehand about any medical conditions or medication, and check with your doctor if you're unsure whether a tattoo is right for you.

How can I plan my session to avoid the crash?#

Book a time when you're rested, fed and free afterwards. For most people, a late-morning or early-afternoon slot before a free evening works well.

  • Choose a day when you don't have to go back to work.
  • Eat a proper meal a couple of hours before.
  • Start a small piece first if you're very nervous.
  • Split very large tattoos into several sessions.

Conclusion: normal, temporary and manageable#

The post-tattoo crash — tiredness, shivers, a low mood or early doubts — is common and usually passes within a day or two. Eat well, hydrate, bring a sweet snack, rest afterwards, and give the tattoo time to heal before you judge it. If you feel faint, feverish or worse over time, see a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

During a session, your body responds to the needle with a rush of adrenaline and endorphins. When they wear off, many people feel drained, sleepy or flat. Long sessions, skipped meals and poor sleep make it stronger.

Mild shivering or feeling cold right after a session is fairly common and usually settles with food, water, warmth and rest. If you have a fever, chills that last, or feel increasingly unwell, see a doctor.

Early doubts are common, especially with a first or visible tattoo. A fresh tattoo is red, swollen and darker than it will be once healed, so it can look very different from what you imagined. These feelings often fade as it heals. If they don't, talk to your artist about options.

A proper meal a couple of hours before your session, with water through the day. Bring a sweet snack such as fruit, juice or a biscuit for longer sittings, and avoid alcohol for 24 hours beforehand.

If you can, go home and rest instead, especially after a long or first session. Plan your appointment so you have a quiet evening afterwards.

See a doctor if you faint or nearly faint, have a fever or chills that don't settle, feel increasingly unwell, or notice spreading redness, worsening pain, swelling, pus or red streaks from the tattoo.
