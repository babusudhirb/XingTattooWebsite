Sibling & Family Tattoos: Best Matching Design Ideas

Peace (Chingri Awungshi)28 Oct 2026 8 min read

The best sibling tattoos share one theme but are sized and placed to suit each person's body. Ideas that age well and avoid cliché include birth flowers, coordinates of a family home, puzzle pieces that connect, a parent's handwriting, or one symbol split across everyone. Book the visit together, then heal and look after the tattoos together.

Key takeaways

  • Match the theme, not necessarily the exact size or spot — each body needs its own fit.
  • Birth flowers, coordinates, handwriting and connecting symbols feel personal without being cheesy.
  • One artist does each tattoo in turn, so plan a group visit with time for everyone.
  • Agree on spelling, dates and design together before anyone sits in the chair.
  • Heal together: share an aftercare routine and remind each other for the first 30 days.

A sibling or family tattoo is a small, permanent way of saying "we belong together". It might mark a wedding, a parent's milestone birthday, a reunion after years apart, or simply a bond you want to carry with you. The trick is choosing sibling and family tattoos that feel meaningful for decades — not a cheesy design you'll cringe at in five years.

This guide shares matching ideas that age well, how to size one theme to suit different bodies, how to book a group visit at Xing Premium Tattoo Studio in Brookefield, and how to look after your new tattoos together. If you're planning tattoos with a partner rather than family, see our guide to couple tattoo ideas.

What makes a good sibling tattoo?#

A good sibling tattoo shares one theme but fits each person. It should mean something to everyone involved, work at a small size, and look good on its own as well as side by side.

Ask yourselves:

  • Would each of us still like this design on our own?
  • Does it refer to something only we understand?
  • Will it suit different skin tones, body shapes and placements?
  • Is it simple enough to stay crisp at a small size?

What are non-cheesy matching tattoo ideas for siblings?#

Ideas that refer to something specific — a place, a person, a memory — age better than generic symbols. Here are some favourites:

Puzzle pieces Only complete together Each sibling has one piece

How do birth flower tattoos work for siblings?#

Each sibling gets the flower of their birth month, drawn in the same style. Side by side, they read as a set; alone, each one is personal.

Ways to style them:

  • Single-stem fine line — delicate and timeless.
  • Small bouquet — one sibling's tattoo combines everyone's flowers.
  • Same frame, different flower — a circle or oval with each person's bloom.

Our minimalist tattoo ideas show how simple floral work looks at small sizes.

How do I size one theme for different bodies?#

Size the design to each person's placement rather than forcing everyone into the same measurement. A wrist, forearm and ankle all need slightly different proportions.

What changes from person to person:

  • Size — a larger arm can take a slightly bigger version.
  • Angle — the design may tilt to follow each wrist or arm.
  • Line weight — finer on smaller placements, a touch bolder on larger.
  • Detail — simplified slightly for very small areas.

What stays the same:

  • The core shape or symbol.
  • The style and font.
  • The overall feel.

Peace's tip: For group tattoos, I draw one master design, then fit it to each person on the day. Same story, but every tattoo sits naturally on its own body.

Where should family tattoos go?#

Popular placements are the inner wrist, forearm, ankle, behind the ear and collarbone. You don't all have to choose the same spot.

  • Inner wrist — great for photos holding hands; thin skin, so keep it simple.
  • Forearm — more room for text or flowers.
  • Ankle — discreet and easy to hide.
  • Collarbone — elegant for a short word.
  • Upper arm — easy to cover for work.

If someone in the family is client-facing at work, a covered placement may suit them better. Our tattoo pain chart helps you compare comfort.

How do we agree on a design without arguing?#

Start with the meaning, not the picture. Once everyone agrees on what the tattoo should say, choosing the design becomes much easier.

  1. Each person shares a memory or word that represents the family.
  2. Find the common thread — a place, a person, a phrase.
  3. Collect three or four reference images together.
  4. Let the artist sketch options at the consultation.
  5. Vote on details like font, size and placement.
  6. Allow small personal differences — that's what keeps everyone happy.

How do we heal our tattoos together?#

Share the routine. Remind each other to wash, moisturise and stay out of the sun, and compare progress as you heal over the first 30 days.

Shared aftercare plan:

  • Wash gently two to three times a day for the first couple of weeks.
  • Pat dry and apply a thin layer of aftercare product.
  • Don't pick or scratch — remind each other.
  • Avoid swimming, saunas and sun while healing.
  • Send each other healing photos in your family group chat.

Our tattoo aftercare guide gives the full day-by-day timeline, and our guide to tattoo healing stages explains what's normal.

What about memorial family tattoos?#

Many families get matching tattoos to honour a parent or grandparent. A shared signature, a date, a favourite flower or a few words in their handwriting can be very moving.

Our guide to memorial tattoo ideas shares respectful ways to remember someone together.

Can friends get "family" tattoos too?#

Of course. Chosen family counts. Friend groups, college roommates and colleagues who've been through a lot together often choose matching pieces. The same rules apply: pick something specific to your shared story, keep it simple, and size it to each person.

Ideas that suit friend groups:

  • A small symbol linked to where you met.
  • A date written in Roman numerals.
  • A shared in-joke turned into a tiny line drawing.
  • Matching symbols in a script none of you speaks — but check the meaning carefully first.

What mistakes should we avoid?#

The biggest mistakes are rushing, misspelling and shrinking the design too much. All three are easy to avoid with a consultation.

  • Choosing a design on the day without everyone agreeing.
  • Copying a design without checking its meaning.
  • Getting dates or names wrong.
  • Going too small to save time.
  • Pressuring a reluctant family member to join in.
