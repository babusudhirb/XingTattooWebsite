Healing Guide

Geometric Aftercare Timeline

Every style heals a little differently. Here's exactly what to expect with your geometric tattoo, stage by stage.

Day 1–3

Fresh & Fragile

  • Clean gently with fragrance-free soap, twice a day
  • Avoid stretching the skin excessively — lines can distort while healing
  • Apply a thin layer of aftercare ointment

Day 4–10

The Peel

  • Light flaking along thin lines is normal
  • Don't pick — line breaks and gaps are the #1 geometric touch-up request
  • Keep the area clean and lightly moisturised

Week 2–3

The Itch

  • Keep skin moisturised so lines don't crack from over-drying
  • Avoid direct sun, pools and saunas until fully healed

Month 1+

Settle & Shine

  • SPF keeps crisp black lines from turning grey or blue over the years
  • Book a touch-up if any line looks broken or faded

General guidance only — always follow the printed aftercare sheet given after your session, and contact us if anything looks infected rather than simply healing.

How long does a geometric or mandala tattoo take?

A geometric or mandala tattoo at Xing Premium Tattoo Studio in Brookefield, Bengaluru, usually takes 1.5–4 hours, depending on size, detail and placement.

Exact symmetry needs careful stencil placement before tattooing starts, so allow extra time for that. Sleeves and large patterns are split across several sessions.

Peace (Chingri Awungshi) gives you a time estimate at your free consultation. Breaks are built in, and you can pause whenever you need to. Eat a proper meal beforehand and keep the day free so nothing feels rushed.

Good To Know

Frequently asked questions

Forearms, upper arms, chest, back and calves work well because the skin there stays relatively stable, which keeps straight lines and symmetry from distorting. Areas that bend a lot, like joints, can stretch a pattern and may need a touch-up. Peace places the stencil first so you can check the alignment before tattooing starts.

When done at the correct needle depth with quality ink, geometric dotwork and linework age crisply and hold their structure well. Very small, tightly packed details can soften over the years, so patterns are sized with some breathing room. Sunscreen once healed and following the aftercare guidance are the biggest factors in keeping lines sharp.

Smaller geometric pieces make a good first tattoo: shorter sessions, moderate discomfort and a bold, clean look. A simple armband, a single mandala or a small sacred-geometry symbol are easy places to start. A geometric piece can also be added to later, so it can grow into a larger design over time.
