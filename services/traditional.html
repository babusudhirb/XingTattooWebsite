Why Choose Indian Traditional Tattoos?

Indian traditional tattoos are more than decoration — they carry devotion, protection and identity. That demands accurate iconography, correct script, and a respectful approach to design and placement.

Healing Guide

Indian Traditional Aftercare Timeline

Every style heals a little differently. Here's exactly what to expect with your indian traditional tattoo, stage by stage.

Day 1–3

Fresh & Fragile

  • Keep the wrap on for the time your artist specifies
  • Wash gently twice a day with fragrance-free soap — pat, don't wipe
  • Apply a thin layer of aftercare ointment as directed

Day 4–14

The Peel

  • Light flaking and scabbing are normal — do not pick
  • Picking over script or fine detail is the #1 cause of gaps and touch-ups
  • Wear loose clothing over the area

Week 2–4

The Itch

  • The tattoo can look dull while new skin forms — true contrast returns after the peel
  • Avoid direct sun, pools and saunas
  • Keep the area lightly moisturised

Long Term

Settle & Shine

  • Daily SPF keeps linework and script crisp for decades
  • Book your free touch-up if any line or letter looks uneven

General guidance only — always follow the printed aftercare sheet given after your session, and contact us if anything looks infected rather than simply healing.

How long does an Indian traditional tattoo take?

An Indian traditional tattoo at Xing Premium Tattoo Studio in Brookefield, Bengaluru, usually takes 1.5–4 hours, depending on size, detail and placement.

A small Om or Trishul is at the shorter end; a detailed deity piece with mandala or lotus work takes longer and may be split into two sessions.

Peace (Chingri Awungshi) gives you a time estimate at your free consultation. Breaks are built in, and you can pause whenever you need to. Eat a proper meal beforehand and keep the day free so nothing feels rushed.
