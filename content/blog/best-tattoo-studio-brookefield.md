## 1. A portfolio of real work

The portfolio is the single best predictor of what your tattoo will look like.

**What to look for:**

- Photos of real clients, ideally with the studio's watermark or a consistent setting.
- Work in the style you want — realism, fine line, lettering, geometric or Indian traditional.
- Healed photos, not just fresh ones. Fresh tattoos are red and sharp; healed ones show the truth.
- Consistency. One great photo is luck. Fifty good ones are skill.

**What to be careful about:**

- Pictures that look like they were downloaded from the internet.
- Heavy filters that hide blurry lines or patchy shading.
- A portfolio that is all one style when you want something completely different.

## 2. The artist's experience

Ask who will actually tattoo you, and how long they have been working.

Experience shows up in small things: steady lines, even shading, clean healing, and good judgement about what will and won't work on your body.

It also shows in what an artist says no to. An experienced artist will tell you when a design is too small to hold detail, or when a placement will fade fast.

In some studios, a senior artist consults and a junior tattoos. That's not always bad, but you should know who is holding the machine.

## 3. Sterilisation and single-use needles

This is the most important check on the list. Poor hygiene can cause infections and spread blood-borne diseases.

At a minimum, a safe studio should use:

- **Single-use needles and cartridges**, opened from sealed packs in front of you.
- **Fresh gloves**, changed whenever the artist touches anything outside the work area.
- **Barrier film** on the machine, cables, bottles and work surfaces.
- **Single-use ink caps**, filled fresh for each client and thrown away after.
- **Sterilised reusable tools**, using a proper medical-grade process.
- **A cleaned, re-wrapped station** between every client.

If you want the full detail, our [hygienic tattoo studio checklist](/blog/hygienic-tattoo-studio-checklist) breaks down every step and the questions to ask.

> **Peace's tip:** Watch the artist set up. If you don't see the needle come out of a sealed pack, ask. A good artist will open a fresh one for you without any fuss.

## 4. Custom design vs copy-paste

A tattoo should be made for you, not printed from a search result.

Custom design means the artist takes your idea, references and placement, then sketches a piece that fits your body's shape. The size, flow and detail are planned around you.

Copy-paste tattoos are designs taken straight from the internet. They often don't fit the placement, may copy another artist's work, and can look identical to thousands of others.

Ask the studio:

- Will the design be drawn from scratch?
- Can I see the design and request changes before the session?
- Will you size the stencil on my body first?

## 5. Consultation quality

A consultation tells you a lot about how a studio works.

A good consultation is:

- **Free and unhurried.** You shouldn't feel rushed into booking.
- **Honest.** The artist explains what will work, what won't, and why.
- **Practical.** You discuss size, placement, colour, sessions and aftercare.
- **Respectful.** Your idea, budget and concerns are taken seriously.

Bring references, know your rough size, and note your questions in advance. Our [first tattoo guide for Bengaluru](/blog/first-tattoo-guide-bangalore) has a list of what to ask.

If the studio pushes you to pay immediately or dismisses your questions, that's a sign to keep looking.

## 6. Reviews — and how to read them

Google reviews are useful, but only if you read them properly.

**Look past the star rating.** A {{stars}} rating is a good sign, but the comments tell you why. Read the most recent reviews, and read a few low ones too.

**Look for patterns:**

- Do people mention cleanliness and single-use needles?
- Do they say the artist was patient and explained aftercare?
- Do they talk about how the tattoo healed, weeks later?
- Do they mention first tattoos, nervousness, or being made comfortable?

**Be cautious of:**

- Many reviews posted on the same day with very similar wording.
- Reviews that say nothing specific at all.
- A studio that argues rudely with unhappy reviewers.

## 7. Quality, sealed inks

Ink goes into your skin and stays there. Ask what the studio uses.

A careful studio will tell you where its inks come from and keep them sealed until use. Inks should be poured into fresh single-use caps for each client, never used straight from a shared bottle with the needle.

If you have sensitive skin or known allergies, mention it during the consultation. If you have a medical condition, check with your doctor before booking.

## 8. Aftercare support

A tattoo isn't finished when you leave the chair. The next few weeks of healing decide how it will look for years.

A good studio will:

- Explain aftercare clearly before you leave.
- Give you written or WhatsApp instructions to follow at home.
- Answer questions while you heal.
- Tell you when to see a doctor instead of waiting it out.
- Offer a touch-up if a small area heals unevenly.

## 9. Red flags — when to walk away

Some warning signs mean you should leave, no matter how good the price or how close the studio is.

| Red flag | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Needles or cartridges not opened in front of you | You can't be sure they are new |
| No gloves, or gloves not changed | Cross-contamination risk |
| Dirty or cluttered workstation | Poor hygiene habits |
| No questions about health or allergies | Your safety isn't a priority |
| Tattooing people who are visibly drunk | Unprofessional and unsafe |
| Pressure to book or pay on the spot | Sales come before your interests |
| Refuses to show more of their work | The portfolio may not be theirs |
| Says any design will work anywhere | Not being honest about limits |

Trust your instinct. If something feels off, it probably is.

## Questions to ask on your first visit

Walking into a tattoo studio for the first time can feel intimidating. Having a few questions ready makes it easier.

**About the artist:**

- Who will do my tattoo, and how long have they been tattooing?
- Can I see more work in this style, including healed photos?

**About the design:**

- Will the design be drawn for me from scratch?
- How many changes can I ask for before the session?
- Will this design work at the size and placement I want?

**About safety:**

- Are needles and cartridges single-use, and will I see them opened?
- How are reusable tools sterilised?
- What inks do you use?

**About aftercare:**

- What aftercare do you recommend?
- Can I message you if I'm worried while it heals?
- Do you offer touch-ups if a small area heals unevenly?

A professional tattoo shop in AECS Layout, Whitefield or anywhere else should answer every one of these clearly and without irritation.
