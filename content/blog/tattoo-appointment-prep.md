Knowing how to prepare for a tattoo makes the whole experience easier. Sleep well, eat a proper meal, stay hydrated, skip alcohol, wear comfortable clothing, bring your ID and references, and arrive with your questions ready. Good preparation helps you sit comfortably and heal better.

At our studio in Brookefield, Bengaluru, we've seen how much a little preparation helps — especially for first-timers. Here's our 12-point checklist.

## How to prepare for a tattoo: the 12-point checklist

Work through these steps in the days before your appointment.

### 1. Sleep well the night before

A rested body handles pain and long sessions far better. Aim for a full night's sleep. Being tired lowers your pain tolerance and makes it harder to sit still.

### 2. Eat a proper meal

Eat a balanced meal one to two hours before your session. Low blood sugar can make you feel dizzy, shaky or faint in the chair. Bring a small snack for sessions longer than two hours.

### 3. Stay hydrated

Drink plenty of water for a day or two before your appointment. Well-hydrated skin is easier to tattoo and often heals better.

### 4. Avoid alcohol and blood thinners — ask your doctor

**Skip alcohol** for at least 24 hours before your tattoo. It can thin your blood and increase bleeding during the session.

**Medication:** some medicines, including aspirin and other blood thinners, can increase bleeding. Don't stop any prescribed medication on your own. **Check with your doctor** before your session if you take regular medicine or have a medical condition.

Also avoid heavy caffeine right before your session if it makes you jittery.

### 5. Wear the right clothing

Choose clothes that give easy access to the placement:

- **Forearm or wrist:** short sleeves or loose sleeves you can roll up.
- **Upper arm or shoulder:** sleeveless top or a loose T-shirt.
- **Back:** a top you can remove or wear backwards; women may prefer a halter or strapless top.
- **Ribs:** a loose top or button-down.
- **Thigh or calf:** shorts or loose trousers.
- **Ankle:** sandals and loose bottoms.

Wear dark or old clothes — ink can stain fabric. Bring a loose, clean layer to wear home over the fresh tattoo.

### 6. Prepare your skin

- **Moisturise the area** in the days before your session — healthy skin takes ink better.
- **Avoid sunburn** or tanning on the area.
- **Don't apply heavy lotion or oil** on the day.
- **Don't shave** the area yourself — your artist will do it.
- **Reschedule** if you have a rash, cut, pimple or irritation where the tattoo will go.

### 7. Bring your references and ID

- One to three reference images.
- Any text, with exact spelling, written down.
- A **photo ID** — most studios need to confirm you're 18 or older.

### 8. Know your questions

Write down what you want to ask:

1. How long will the session take?
2. Will the design work at this size and placement?
3. What aftercare do you recommend?
4. How do I contact you if I have questions while healing?
5. Do you offer touch-ups if a small area heals unevenly?

### 9. Plan your day around the session

- Don't book your session right before an important event, wedding or beach holiday.
- Avoid swimming, the gym and long sun exposure for about two weeks afterwards.
- If the session is long, plan a quiet evening to rest.
- Arrange transport home if you prefer not to ride a two-wheeler right after a long session.

### 10. Arrive on time — and relaxed

Arriving late means less time for your tattoo. Arriving rushed makes you tense. Leave a little early, especially with Bengaluru traffic.

### 11. Bring comfort items

- Water and a snack.
- Headphones with music or a podcast.
- A phone charger.
- A light jacket — studios can be cool.
- A friend for support, if the studio allows.

### 12. Be ready for aftercare

Before you leave the studio, make sure you know:

- How long to keep the wrap on.
- How to wash and moisturise.
- Which products to use and avoid.
- What's normal and when to see a doctor.

## What not to do before a tattoo

- Don't get tattooed if you're unwell, feverish or very tired.
- Don't drink alcohol or take recreational drugs.
- Don't go in on an empty stomach.
- Don't apply numbing cream without asking your artist first.
- Don't book right before a big trip or event.

Our [tattoo pain chart](/blog/tattoo-pain-chart) explains how to manage pain during the session.

## Preparing for a long session

For large pieces and sessions of several hours:

- Eat a bigger meal beforehand and bring more snacks.
- Wear extra-comfortable clothes.
- Plan breaks with your artist.
- Arrange to rest for the rest of the day.

## If you feel nervous

Nerves are completely normal, especially before a first tattoo. A few things help:

- **Talk to your artist.** Tell them you're nervous — a good artist will explain each step.
- **Ask for a test line.** Feeling the needle for a second removes the fear of the unknown.
- **Breathe slowly** and steadily throughout.
- **Take breaks** whenever you need them.
- **Bring a friend** for support if the studio allows.

Most people find the anticipation is worse than the tattoo itself.

## Preparing for a cover-up or touch-up

If you're coming in for a cover-up or touch-up, bring clear daylight photos of the old tattoo, tell your artist how it healed originally, and make sure the area is fully healed and free of irritation. Our guide to [tattoo cover-ups](/blog/tattoo-cover-up-guide) explains what to expect.
