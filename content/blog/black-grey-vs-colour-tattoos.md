A black and grey tattoo usually ages better than a colour tattoo. Black ink holds strongly in the skin and suits almost every skin tone, while colours — especially light shades — soften faster and need more sun protection to stay bright.

That doesn't mean colour is a bad choice. It means each has strengths, and the right one depends on your design, your skin and how you want the tattoo to look years from now.

Here's how we explain it to clients at our studio in Brookefield, Bengaluru.

## What a black and grey tattoo is

Black and grey tattoos use black ink in different strengths. The artist dilutes black into soft greys to build shading, from deep shadow to pale highlight.

This style creates:

- **Smooth gradients** that give depth and form.
- **Strong contrast** between darks and bare skin.
- **A timeless, photographic feel**, a bit like a black-and-white photograph.

It's the go-to choice for realism, portraits, statues, deities, animals and many spiritual designs.

## What a colour tattoo is

Colour tattoos use pigments across the spectrum — reds, blues, greens, yellows, purples and more. They can be:

- **Bold and saturated**, like traditional or neo-traditional styles.
- **Soft and blended**, like watercolour.
- **Realistic**, with colour used to recreate life-like tones.
- **Accent-based**, with mostly black work and a single colour highlight.

## How each style ages

All tattoos soften slightly over the years. Ink particles spread a little under the skin, and the skin itself changes with age and sun.

**Black and grey ageing:**

- Black holds well for a long time.
- Very soft grey shading can fade gently, which is why contrast matters.
- Well-designed pieces tend to look good for decades.

**Colour ageing:**

- Strong, dark colours like deep red, blue and green hold reasonably well.
- Light colours — yellow, pastel pink, light blue and white — usually soften first.
- Sun exposure speeds up colour fading more than anything else.

## Tattoo fading: what actually causes it

Tattoo fading isn't just about ink. Several things work together:

1. **Sun.** UV light breaks down ink over time. This is the biggest factor.
2. **Skin type and age.** Skin renews itself and changes with age.
3. **Placement.** Hands, feet and areas that rub against clothes fade faster.
4. **Aftercare.** Picking, over-soaking or sun on a healing tattoo can cause early fading.
5. **Technique.** Ink placed at the right depth and well saturated lasts longer.
6. **Design.** Small, very detailed designs blur sooner than bold ones.

You can't stop ageing completely, but you can slow it down a lot with sunscreen and good aftercare.

## Skin tone considerations

India has an incredible range of skin tones, and that matters when choosing ink.

Tattoo ink sits under the top layer of skin. Your natural skin tone acts like a tinted window over it.

- **Black and grey** shows clearly on light, medium and deep skin tones.
- **Deep, rich colours** — red, maroon, deep blue, green, purple — generally show well on medium to darker skin.
- **Light colours** — yellow, pastel shades and white — can look subtle or barely visible on deeper skin tones.

A good artist will be honest about which colours will show on your skin and which might disappear. Our guide to [tattoos on Indian skin tones](/blog/tattoos-on-indian-skin) goes deeper into this.

> **Peace's tip:** If you love colour but aren't sure how it will look on you, ask for a small colour accent in a mostly black and grey piece. A red flower, a blue eye or a gold detail can give you the pop without the commitment.

## Touch-up needs

Both styles may need a touch-up at some point, but colour usually needs it sooner.

- **Black and grey:** a refresh of highlights or deepening of blacks after many years, if needed.
- **Colour:** brightening lighter shades every few years, especially on sun-exposed areas.

## Combining both: black and grey with colour accents

You don't have to pick only one. Many of the most striking tattoos mix both.

**Popular combinations:**

- A black and grey portrait with coloured eyes.
- A black and grey samurai or warrior with red accents.
- A grey lotus with a soft pink centre.
- A black geometric design with a single colour inside one shape.

Mixed pieces can give you the longevity of black and grey with a focal point of colour that draws the eye.

## How to decide

Ask yourself:

1. **What's the subject?** Portraits, statues and deities usually suit black and grey. Flowers and nature can shine in colour.
2. **What's my skin tone?** Choose colours that will show clearly.
3. **Where will it go?** Sun-exposed spots fade faster, especially in colour.
4. **How much upkeep do I want?** Black and grey needs less.
5. **What do I want it to look like in ten years?** Picture it then, not just now.

If you're still unsure, ask your artist to sketch both versions. Seeing them side by side usually makes the choice clear.

## Sun care for every tattoo

Whichever style you choose, the sun is your tattoo's biggest enemy.

- Keep a healing tattoo completely out of direct sun — use clothing, not sunscreen.
- Once healed, use a broad-spectrum sunscreen on exposed tattoos every day.
- Reapply after sweating or swimming.
- Cover up with light sleeves on long two-wheeler rides.

Bengaluru's pleasant weather can trick you into skipping sunscreen. UV is still strong, even on cloudy days.
