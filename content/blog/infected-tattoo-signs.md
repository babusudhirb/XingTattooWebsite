Knowing the infected tattoo signs can protect your health. Normal healing brings some redness, swelling and tenderness in the first few days. Warning signs are different: redness that spreads or worsens, increasing pain, pus or a bad smell, red streaks, or fever. If you notice any of these, see a doctor promptly.

This post gives general information only. It isn't medical advice. If you're worried about your tattoo, always consult a doctor.

At our studio in Brookefield, Bengaluru, we'd always rather you check than wait. Here's how to tell the difference.

## Normal healing: what to expect

Most tattoos heal without problems. In the first few days, it's normal to have:

- **Redness** close to the lines that fades day by day.
- **Mild swelling** that eases within a few days.
- **Warmth** on the first day or two.
- **Clear fluid**, a little blood and excess ink in the first 24 hours.
- **Tenderness**, like a mild sunburn.

Later, from around day four to fourteen:

- **Peeling** and light scabbing.
- **Itching**.
- **A dull, cloudy look** as new skin forms.

Our guide to [tattoo healing stages](/blog/tattoo-healing-stages) explains each stage in detail.

## Infected tattoo signs: the warning signs

These signs suggest something may be wrong. See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- **Spreading redness** that extends beyond the tattoo or keeps getting worse after the first few days.
- **Increasing pain** instead of gradually easing.
- **Swelling that grows** rather than settles.
- **Pus**, yellow or green discharge, or a bad smell.
- **Red streaks** spreading out from the tattoo.
- **Hot, hard skin** around the area.
- **Fever, chills** or feeling generally unwell.
- **Open sores** or blisters on the tattoo.

The [American Academy of Dermatology](https://www.aad.org/news/tattoo-complications) notes that infections are most likely in the first days or weeks after getting a tattoo, and advises seeing a doctor right away if you have redness and pain around the tattoo, drainage, crusting or pus.

## Normal vs warning signs at a glance

| Sign | Usually normal | See a doctor promptly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Redness | Close to lines, fading | Spreading or worsening after day three |
| Swelling | Mild, easing | Increasing |
| Fluid | Clear, first day | Pus, coloured discharge, bad smell |
| Pain | Tender, easing | Getting worse |
| Itching | During peeling | With spreading rash, bumps or blisters |
| Temperature | Warm on day one | Fever, chills |
| Lines from tattoo | None | Red streaks |

## Allergic reactions

Not every problem is an infection. Some people react to ingredients in tattoo ink, aftercare products or even the gloves used during tattooing.

**Signs of an allergic reaction can include:**

- Itchy, raised bumps on the tattoo.
- A rash, often in one colour of the tattoo.
- Swelling or scaly skin.

Allergic reactions can appear soon after the tattoo or much later — months or even years. The American Academy of Dermatology advises seeing a board-certified dermatologist, rather than a tattoo artist, for these later changes.

**If you have trouble breathing, swelling of the face or lips, or feel faint, seek emergency medical care immediately.**

## When to see a doctor — immediately

Don't wait to see if it gets better. See a doctor promptly if:

- You have any of the warning signs above.
- You have a fever after getting a tattoo.
- You have diabetes, a weakened immune system or another condition that affects healing, and the tattoo doesn't look right.
- You're worried that equipment may not have been sterile. A doctor can advise you on tests for blood-borne infections.

Tell the doctor when and where you got the tattoo, and bring any aftercare instructions you were given.

> **Peace's tip:** If you're unsure whether something is normal, send your studio a clear photo in daylight — but if you see pus, spreading redness, red streaks or have a fever, go to a doctor first. Your health always comes before the tattoo.

## Don't self-treat

When a tattoo looks wrong, it's tempting to try something at home. Please don't.

- **Don't apply** turmeric, toothpaste, antiseptic liquids, leftover creams or home remedies.
- **Don't take** leftover antibiotics or someone else's medicine.
- **Don't pick** or squeeze the area.
- **Don't cover it tightly** with plastic.
- **Don't keep using** products that seem to make it worse.

A doctor will decide on the right treatment.

## Prevention: how to lower the risk

Most tattoo infections can be prevented with two things: a hygienic studio and careful aftercare.

**At the studio:**

- Choose a studio that uses single-use needles opened in front of you.
- Look for gloves, barrier film, single-use ink caps and sterilised tools.
- Make sure the station is cleaned and re-wrapped between clients.

Our [hygienic tattoo studio checklist](/blog/hygienic-tattoo-studio-checklist) explains what to look for.

**At home:**

- Wash your hands before touching the tattoo.
- Wash the tattoo gently with mild, fragrance-free soap.
- Pat dry with a clean paper towel.
- Apply only a thin layer of the recommended product.
- Wear clean, loose clothing.
- Avoid swimming, saunas and soaking for at least two weeks.
- Keep pets away from the fresh tattoo.

Our [tattoo aftercare guide](/blog/tattoo-aftercare-guide) covers the full routine.

## Who should be extra careful

Speak to your doctor before getting a tattoo if you have:

- Diabetes or slow-healing skin.
- A weakened immune system.
- A heart condition.
- A bleeding disorder, or if you take blood thinners.
- A skin condition in the area you want tattooed.

A responsible studio will ask about your health before tattooing.

## What a doctor may ask

If you visit a doctor about your tattoo, it helps to have answers ready:

- When and where did you get the tattoo?
- When did the symptoms start, and are they getting better or worse?
- What aftercare products have you used?
- Do you have a fever or feel unwell?
- Do you have any allergies or medical conditions?
- Do you know whether single-use needles were used?

Bring photos of the tattoo from each day if you have them. They help the doctor see how it has changed.

## After treatment

Follow your doctor's advice fully, even if the tattoo starts to look better. Once it has healed, the tattoo may look patchy where infection affected the skin. Talk to your artist about a touch-up only after your doctor confirms the skin has recovered.
