## Constellations

A constellation tattoo shows the stars that form your sign — dots joined by fine lines.

**Why people love them:**

- Minimal and elegant.
- Personal without being obvious.
- Easy to place almost anywhere.

**Style tips:**

- Use slightly larger dots for the brightest stars.
- Give the pattern enough space so dots don't merge as the tattoo ages.
- Add a tiny moon or star accent for interest.

**Placements:** behind the ear, collarbone, wrist, spine, shoulder blade.

## Glyph vs illustrative

| | Glyph | Illustrative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Look | Simple symbol | A picture of the sign's creature or object |
| Size | Small | Medium to large |
| Style | Minimal, fine line | Realism, line art, black and grey |
| Best for | First tattoos, subtle placements | Statement pieces |
| Examples | ♌ for Leo | A lion portrait |

Many people combine both — an illustration with the glyph tucked into the design.

> **Peace's tip:** If you want a zodiac sign that feels truly yours, pair it with something from your own life — your birth flower, your initials or the date. That's what turns a common sign into a unique tattoo.

## Zodiac and Indian astrology

Some clients prefer signs from Indian astrology (Jyotish), where signs are known as rashis — for example, Mesha for Aries or Simha for Leo. Dates can differ from Western astrology. If you follow Indian astrology, confirm your rashi with someone you trust before choosing your design. You can also include the rashi name in Devanagari or Kannada script — check spelling carefully.

## Keeping small zodiac tattoos sharp

- Avoid making constellations too tiny.
- Choose slightly bolder lines for glyphs on the wrist or fingers.
- Protect healed tattoos with sunscreen.
- Follow aftercare closely.

## Birth flowers to pair with your sign

Many people pair their zodiac sign with their birth month flower. A few common pairings: carnation for January, violet for February, daffodil for March, daisy for April, lily of the valley for May, rose for June, water lily or lotus for July, poppy for August, morning glory for September, marigold for October, chrysanthemum for November and narcissus for December. Combining a glyph or constellation with your birth flower makes a widely shared sign uniquely yours. Flower meanings vary between sources, so choose the bloom that feels right to you rather than worrying about a single correct list.
